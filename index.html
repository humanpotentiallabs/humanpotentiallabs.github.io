About the Labs

Meditation, breathwork, and holistic lifestyle practices have powerful potential to improve not only mental and physical health but human potential, yet a lack of rigorous scientific evidence hinders mainstream adoption.

Human Potential Labs will close that gap by generating world-class evidence, training the next generation of scientists, and translating findings into offerings that reach communities.

No academic entity is better positioned than Mass General Brigham to rigorously evaluate, translate, and scale evidence-based approaches to whole-person well-being and human flourishing. The initiative unites two internationally recognized assets: the Benson-Henry Institute for Mind Body Medicine at Massachusetts General Hospital and the Osher Center for Integrative Health at Harvard Medical School and Brigham and Women’s Hospital.

Our mission

Three integrated aims guide the work of Human Potential Labs.

  1. 01

    Scientific discovery

    Advance understanding of how breathwork, meditation, and holistic lifestyle practices affect well-being and human flourishing, using state-of-the-art technology and methodology.

  2. 02

    Research to practice

    Integrate proven interventions into mainstream care so they are universally recognized, accepted, and accessible.

  3. 03

    Societal impact

    Inform policy by engaging the public and policymakers and sharing evidence through media, publications, and outreach.

Expected impact

  • Accelerate discoveries that reframe how well-being is understood and measured
  • Bring rigorously validated approaches to patients worldwide
  • Inform healthcare policy and reimbursement decisions
  • Train future leaders in the science of human flourishing
  • Establish a lasting Harvard-based center of excellence for advancing human potential through ancient and modern practices

Inspiration

Human Potential Labs is inspired by the work of The Art of Living Foundation and its founder, Gurudev Sri Sri Ravi Shankar.

For more than four decades, Gurudev has shown that simple practices of breathwork and meditation can help people everywhere meet stress with resilience and live with greater clarity, joy, and purpose. Central to this work is Sudarshan Kriya Yoga (SKY), a rhythmic breathing practice he developed that has become the subject of a growing body of scientific research.

His vision of uniting ancient wisdom with modern science shapes the question at the heart of the Labs: what is the full potential of a human being, and how can rigorous evidence help more people realize it?

Research areas

Research in the first three years is organized in three complementary areas that differ in scientific risk, population reach, and time to result. The framework was developed in consultation with the directors of the Benson-Henry Institute and the Osher Center.

Research area 1

A first-of-its-kind database of exceptional individuals

Rigorous documentation of gifted individuals who demonstrate heightened perceptual, decision-making, and healing abilities, grounded in mainstream cognitive and perceptual science, with standardized protocols and independent verification.

Highest attention value; smallest sample sizes.

Research area 2

Population-level dissemination

Breathwork, meditation, and holistic lifestyle interventions delivered at scale in educational institutions and broader communities, building on foundations already in place, including work in addiction recovery and healthcare-provider burnout.

Nearest-term outputs, clearest policy relevance, and the strongest potential for extramural funding.

Research area 3

Mechanistic deep dive

Genomics and metabolomics, wearables, biophysics, and advanced neuroimaging applied to experienced versus novice practitioners, to establish what both sustained and early stages of practice do at the level of biology.

The most technically demanding area, and the work most likely to change how the field is understood.

Research network

Human Potential Labs is designed as a coordinated network rather than a single facility. Participating sites operate as pods under a single administrative home, with shared protocols, harmonized measurements, and a unified data architecture.

From evidence to practice

Each study is designed with a defined path to adoption, moving validated findings into care, education, and institutional policy.

  • Clinical integration

    Validated programs embedded in Mass General Brigham care pathways through the Benson-Henry Institute and the Osher Center.

  • Workforce and education

    Clinician training, Harvard Medical School curricula, and well-being programs for Mass General Brigham staff.

  • Value evidence

    Cost-effectiveness and budget-impact analyses to inform health-system leaders and payers.

  • Institutional scale

    Standardized protocols and digital delivery to extend proven programs across Mass General Brigham and into communities.

Support the Labs

A founding gift will establish Human Potential Labs and fund its first three years. The plan is deliberately lean, drawing on existing Mass General Brigham space, computing, and administrative resources. Additional philanthropy will scale research across all three areas, expand pilot grants and the research network, and secure the Labs’ long-term future.
